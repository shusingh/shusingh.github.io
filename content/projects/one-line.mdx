## System intent

One Line is a journal built around a single constraint: you write exactly one sentence a day and pick a mood. The constraint is the product. Ten seconds of effort is small enough to survive contact with real life, and after a few weeks the app starts paying you back, resurfacing what you wrote a week, a month, and a year ago today.

It is deliberately not a notes app, not a habit platform, and not a cloud service. There is no backend, no account, and no network dependency for data: the entire journal lives in `localStorage`, with JSON export and import as the escape hatch.

## Requirements

- Writing today's line must take seconds: no login, no navigation, no save anxiety.
- Each day stores at most one entry: a line of text and a mood.
- Old entries resurface on their anniversaries (week, month, year).
- The full year renders as a mood-coloured field of ink droplets.
- Data must be exportable and restorable without any server.
- The visual language extends this site's suminagashi system: washi paper, sumi ink, and traditional pigments.

## High-level design

  nodes={['type one line', 'pick a mood', 'persist to date key', 'derive streaks and stats', 'render flashbacks and year map']}
  caption="Every feature is a pure derivation from one date-keyed map of entries."

The store is a single object mapping `YYYY-MM-DD` keys to `{ text, mood }`. Everything else, streaks, flashbacks, the year map, monthly insight counts, is derived at render time. There is no cache to invalidate and no second source of truth.

## Design decisions

**One entry per day, enforced by the key.** Using the date as the primary key makes the core rule structural rather than validated. Editing is just overwriting today's key.

**Derive, never store, statistics.** Streaks and mood balances are recomputed from the map on every render. The journal is small (a year is a few kilobytes), so correctness wins over caching.

**The mood scale avoids alarm colours.** Moods map to traditional pigments running cool to warm, ending in yamabuki gold rather than red, so a great day never reads as a warning.

**Local-first is a feature, not a fallback.** A journal is among the most private documents a person owns. Keeping it on-device is the honest architecture, and export/import keeps the user in control of backup.

## What I would improve

Backfilling missed days from the year map, an optional encrypted sync target for people with multiple devices, and a small PWA manifest so the journal installs to a phone home screen.
